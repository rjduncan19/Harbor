# Business model & go-to-market

## Competitive wedge

Four existing camps: locked-down senior hardware (GrandPad, RAZ), scam/identity monitoring (Aura, LifeLock — advisory only), remote-support/tracking tools (AirDroid, uMobix — some creepy), and institutional MDM for facilities (SureMDM, Scalefusion, Miradore — B2B).

Harbor's gap: **software** on the parent's **own** device, **enforcement** not alerts, packaged for a **non-technical family member**.

GrandPad (~$40–75+/mo plus hardware) bundles the caregiver portal with a device and connectivity. Harbor sells only the protection-and-management layer.

Platform risk (Apple/Microsoft entering) judged moderate: they avoid adult-surveillance and dignity issues, and Microsoft historically under-invests in family scenarios. Aim at hard caregiver scenarios, not the "bigger font" end.

## Costs

1. Apple: $99/yr developer account for APNs cert; APNs free; Apple Business Manager free but adds ops
2. Hosting: light per device
3. Third-party security services: deferred — lean on free platform protections
4. Trust tax: SOC 2 / certifications (fixed, five figures+/yr)
5. Support: the cost that sets the price floor; contained by the support boundary and warm AI-assisted first line

## Pricing

Target **~$9–15 per device per month**. Not single digits — that undersells trust and starves support.

Positioning is **premium + dignity**, not just undercutting GrandPad: for families who refuse the deficit device.

Staged tiers under the mission model:
- **Premium** — full price; funds the mission
- **Standard** — covers cost plus a little
- **Subsidized / sponsored** — for lower-income seniors, funded by premium margin, grants, partners

## Certifications are a means, not the goal

Customers can't read a SOC 2 report. They trust names like AARP. Certifications are a B2B qualification for the gatekeepers who endorse us. Spend only on what those endorsers require.

## Validator ladder (climb in order; each rung earns the next)

1. **AgeTech accelerator** — e.g. AARP-affiliated AgeTech Collaborative. Mentorship, cohort, intros, early legitimacy.
2. **Senior-advocacy / direct-service nonprofit** — e.g. Meals on Wheels, Area Agency on Aging. Pilots and testimonials; subsidized tier is the entry ticket.
3. **Trust press** — aging-tech reviewers, consumer-safety outlets. Independent validation customers find when they search.
4. **AARP licensing** — crown jewel. A paid royalty licensing arrangement with a high bar (current licensees are large insurers). Pursue after traction and certifications.

The mission identity and the anti-surveillance guarantee are themselves trust assets with these validators.
