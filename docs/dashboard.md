# Adult-child dashboard

**Guiding principle:** forgiving, reassuring, hard to break. Destructive actions have a safety net. The default state says "everything's fine."

## Zones

1. **Reassurance (home):** one glance — is the device online, protected, anything needing me? Calm green by default.
2. **Panic actions:** clear/recover a passcode lockout (signature feature), locate (on-demand, emergency only), lock, ring the device. Big, hard to misfire, easy to undo.
3. **Apps & access:** a request-and-approve inbox ("Mom wants WhatsApp"), never a policy matrix.
4. **Trust surface:** makes invisible protection visible — "this week: 12 scam sites blocked, 3 risky downloads stopped."
5. **Gentle alerts:** text/push only when something genuinely needs the adult child, worded calmly.

## App approval — trust tiers

Not every app should need approval.

| Tier | Behavior |
|---|---|
| Curated safe list (common elder apps: weather, official bank apps, WhatsApp, news) | Auto-approve / zero friction |
| Gray zone (unknown, uncommon) | Gentle request to the adult child |
| Blocked (known-bad, scam-adjacent) | Quietly denied; never reaches the adult child |

Intelligence behind the list, compounding:
- **Expert curation** by Harbor — the trusted final filter
- **Community signal** — what adult children commonly approve across the user base
- **AI risk assessment** of new apps

## Mission scoreboard

Show blocked-threat counts per device **and** aggregated across all Harbor users. Because Harbor is mission-based, the aggregate is proof the mission works — for the website, validator pitches, and press — and lets the adult child feel part of a cause, not just a buyer of a utility. Aggregate counts only; no per-person browsing data (see minimum-data principle).
