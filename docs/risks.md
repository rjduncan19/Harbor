# Risks — how a trust product breaks

Trust is asymmetric: built slowly, lost instantly. Most real risk is Harbor's own failure modes and promises, not the scammers. Each item below needs design work.

1. **A scam gets through and the parent loses money.** It will eventually happen. Promise "dramatically safer," never "perfectly safe." Expectation-setting and liability, not only technology.
2. **Harbor blocks something the parent genuinely needs** (real bank site, needed app, urgent email). Too tight is as dangerous as too loose. Needs fast, forgiving override paths.
3. **Harbor itself is breached.** We hold control over many elders' devices — a master key to a vulnerable population. Existential. Minimum data and the capability boundary shrink the blast radius.
4. **Availability.** An outage during a lockout emergency. The service must work at the moment it's needed.
5. **Dignity / consent failure.** Anything that feels like surveillance, or lets an adult child over-control a competent parent, breaks the core differentiator.
6. **False sense of security.** Families stop paying attention; complacency makes harm worse when something slips through.
